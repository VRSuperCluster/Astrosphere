*MVP Spec v0.2 — Transit Calendar*

**MVP Product Specification**

*Personalised Transit Calendar*

*Astrology as invisible infrastructure. Psychology as the interface.
A personal development tool that happens to be powered by the stars.*

# 1. Product Vision

A web-based personal development tool that translates astrological transit data into psychologically grounded, actionable calendar entries. The user never needs to understand astrology. They experience a timeline of honest, personal insight mapped to their year — delivered in a voice that feels like a friend who studied psychology and happens to know their chart.

The astrology is the engine. The psychology is the interface. The calendar is the delivery mechanism.

**CORE POSITIONING**

Existing astrology apps fall into two camps: full mystical language (Susan Miller, Cafe Astrology) or cold brutalist aesthetics (Co–Star). Neither speaks to the growing audience that is interested in self-awareness tools but turned off by horoscope culture. This product occupies the gap — warm, serif, Japanese-inspired minimalism with a voice that is honest, slightly confrontational, and deeply personal. No zodiac jargon in the output. Astrology in the hashtags and metadata only.

**KEY DIFFERENTIATOR**

Every calendar entry is generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6, synthesising the user’s full natal chart, the specific transit, and their life context answers into a unique interpretation. This is not template-based horoscope content. Two people with the same sun sign but different moon placements, rising signs, and life situations will receive meaningfully different calendar entries. The AI layer is what makes the product feel personal rather than generic — it is the product.

# 2. Brand Identity

**VOICE**

Honest, warm, slightly confrontational. Like a therapist who skips the pleasantries. The tone assumes intelligence, avoids hand-holding, and names the uncomfortable thing directly. It is personal without being precious, actionable without being prescriptive.

*Instead of: **"**Saturn transiting your 7th house — a period of testing in partnerships.**"*

*We say: **"**The next few months are going to pressure-test your closest relationships. You’ll feel the urge to either commit harder or walk away — that tension is the point. Ask yourself: am I staying because this is real, or because leaving feels harder?**"*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**

Serif typography (the product should feel literary, not techy). Japanese-inspired minimalism: generous whitespace, warm paper tones, restraint over decoration. The palette is off-white and warm neutrals with a single muted accent — think Kinfolk magazine meets a personal journal. The entire experience, from an Instagram post to the website to a calendar entry sitting in someone’s Google Calendar, should feel like the same voice and the same warmth.

**CONTENT STRATEGY HOOK**

Social content (Instagram, TikTok) uses the same psychological voice as headlines but includes astrology tags and hashtags for discoverability. The posts read as personal development, the metadata reads as astrology. This self-selects for the right audience: people searching astrology content find something that feels different from everything else in that space.

# 3. MVP Scope

The MVP is scoped to be buildable in two to three focused weeks using Cursor with Claude as the AI coding assistant. It ships the smallest version that delivers real value and feels like the brand from the first interaction. The principle is: a beautifully paced three-step flow that gives you an AI-generated personal calendar is more valuable than a feature-rich tool that feels generic.

**WHAT SHIPS**

Three screens: a multi-step birth data questionnaire, a chart summary page, and a transit calendar download. The questionnaire collects the minimum data needed for accurate chart generation plus two to three optional life context questions. The chart page shows a visual natal chart with key placements described in psychological language. The calendar is a CSV file containing AI-generated personalised transit entries for the next twelve months, importable to Google Calendar or Apple Calendar.

**WHAT DOES NOT SHIP**

User accounts and saved charts, a full interactive chart explorer, payment or premium tiers, the content automation pipeline (n8n, social posting), a mobile app, and the dating/synastry concept. These are all future layers, not MVP requirements.

# 4. User Flow

## Step 1: Birth Data Questionnaire

A clean, single-question-per-screen form inspired by Typeform. The pacing matters — each screen should feel calm and considered, not like a data entry form. Fields collected:

- Date of birth (date picker)

- Time of birth (time picker with an "I don’t know" option)

- Place of birth (location autocomplete for coordinates)

- First name only (used to personalise calendar entries)

- One to two optional context questions (see below)

**OPTIONAL CONTEXT QUESTIONS**

These are not astrological. They add a life-context layer that makes the AI-generated calendar entries feel deeply personal rather than generically insightful. Presented as optional single-select screens after the birth data:

- "What area of your life feels most in motion right now?" — options: Relationships, Career, Identity, Health, Creativity

- "What’s your relationship with change?" — options: I seek it out, I resist it, It depends, I’m in the middle of one

These answers are passed directly to the AI interpretation layer as part of the generation prompt. Unlike a template system where context maps to pre-written variants, the LLM can weave these answers naturally into every interpretation, producing genuinely different output for each combination of chart data and life context.

**UNKNOWN BIRTH TIME**

If the user selects "I don’t know my birth time," the system defaults to a solar chart (sun on the ascendant at noon). The chart page should note this gracefully: "Without an exact birth time, your chart uses a solar view — the broad strokes are accurate, but house placements are approximate." The calendar still generates, just without house-specific transits.

## Step 2: Chart Summary

After submission, the user lands on a chart summary page. This is not a full interactive chart explorer — it is a moment of recognition. The goal is for the user to read two or three sentences and think "how does it know."

**WHAT APPEARS**

A visual natal chart wheel (clean, minimal, matching the brand aesthetic — not the cluttered default astro-chart look). Below it, three to five key placements described in psychological language. These summaries are generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 in the same API call that produces the calendar entries, ensuring consistent voice and cross-referencing between placements. For example, for someone with Sun in Scorpio, Moon in Gemini, and Ascendant in Libra, the summary might read:

*"**You lead with charm and diplomacy — people experience you as easy, balanced, agreeable. Underneath that, you’re running two competing programs: one that needs to control and transform everything it touches, and one that can’t stop asking questions and gets bored the moment it has an answer. The tension between depth and lightness is your operating system.**"*

**EMAIL CAPTURE**

Below the chart summary, before the calendar download, a single email field: "Get your calendar, and we’ll let you know when new transits hit your chart." This is the growth mechanism. No account creation, just an email tied to the chart data for future re-engagement.

## Step 3: Transit Calendar Download

The primary output. A CSV file covering the next twelve months of significant transits to the user’s natal chart, formatted for import into Google Calendar or Apple Calendar.

**CALENDAR ENTRY FORMAT**

Each entry includes a date, a short title (the psychological headline), and a description (two to four sentences of actionable, personal insight). No astrological terminology in the user-facing text. The underlying transit data is used only as the generation key for the AI.

**Example entry:**

**Date:** June 14, 2026

**Title:** Your patience with half-truths runs out today

**Description:** Something you’ve been tolerating in a relationship or at work is going to feel suddenly intolerable. This isn’t impulsive — you’ve been building toward this clarity for weeks. Trust the frustration. It’s telling you where your standards actually are.

**TRANSIT SELECTION FOR MVP**

Not every transit is meaningful enough for a calendar entry. The MVP should include: outer planet transits to natal sun, moon, rising, Mercury, Venus, and Mars (Saturn, Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto transits only — these are the slow-moving, life-chapter transits). Major aspects only: conjunctions, oppositions, and squares. This keeps the calendar meaningful rather than noisy — roughly two to six entries per month depending on the chart.

# 6. Technical Architecture

**STACK**

Next.js (React) deployed on Vercel. This gives server-side rendering for the chart page (good for eventual SEO and social sharing previews), API routes for the backend logic, and a fast deployment pipeline. Built using Cursor with Claude as the AI coding assistant, leveraging Vilim’s existing UX and Framer background.

**DEVELOPMENT ENVIRONMENT**

Cursor provides a visual IDE experience with inline AI assistance, file tree navigation, and change previews before accepting — a better fit for someone coming from a UX and design background than a terminal-native tool. Claude inside Cursor handles scaffolding, iteration, and debugging. The codebase is framework-standard Next.js and can be worked on with any editor later.

**CHART CALCULATION**

Swiss Ephemeris is the standard library for precise astronomical calculations. For a JavaScript/Node environment, the swisseph npm package provides bindings. The calculation flow: take birth date, time, and coordinates from the questionnaire; compute planetary positions, house cusps, and aspects; store the resulting chart data as a JSON object that feeds both the chart visual and the transit engine.

**TRANSIT ENGINE**

A backend function that takes the natal chart JSON and computes upcoming transits for the next twelve months. For each transit that matches the filter criteria (outer planet to personal planet, major aspect), it produces a structured transit object containing the date, the transiting planet, the natal planet being aspected, the aspect type, and the house involved. This structured list is the input payload for the AI interpretation layer.

**AI INTERPRETATION LAYER**

The core of the product. A single API call to Claude Sonnet 4.6 that receives the full natal chart data, the list of upcoming transits, and the user’s context answers, and returns both the chart personality summary and all calendar entries in one structured response.

The system prompt is the most important piece of intellectual property in the product. It defines the voice, the psychological framework, the formatting rules, and includes hand-written gold-standard examples that anchor the tone. The prompt instructs the model to never use astrological terminology in the output, to write in second person, to be specific and confrontational rather than vague and reassuring, and to reference the user’s life context naturally throughout.

The API call requests structured JSON output: an array of calendar entries each containing a date, title, and description, plus a chart summary object. This structured output is parsed directly into the CSV generator and the chart summary page. The entire generation happens server-side in a Next.js API route, so the user sees a loading state while the AI processes.

**COST ANALYSIS**

Each user triggers one API call to Claude Sonnet 4.6. The input is approximately 2,000 tokens (system prompt, chart data, transit list, context answers) and the output is approximately 3,000 tokens (chart summary plus 24–72 calendar entries). At Sonnet 4.6 pricing of roughly $6 per million tokens blended, each user costs approximately $0.03. At 1,000 users per month, the API cost is $30. At 10,000 users per month, it is $300. These costs are negligible against any revenue model and should not constrain product decisions.

**CHART VISUAL**

A server-rendered SVG chart wheel. Minimal, brand-consistent: warm tones, serif labels, clean lines. This does not need to be interactive for the MVP — a static SVG generated from the chart data and rendered on the summary page. Libraries like d3.js or a custom SVG builder can handle this.

**HOSTING AND INFRASTRUCTURE**

Vercel for the Next.js app (free tier is sufficient for launch). No database required for the MVP — chart data exists only in the session between form submission and download. Email capture can go to a simple service (Mailchimp, Buttondown, or even a Google Sheet via API). If session persistence becomes needed, a lightweight Supabase or PlanetScale instance can be added later.

# 7. System Prompt Architecture

The system prompt is the single highest-leverage asset in the product. It replaces what would otherwise be ninety-plus hand-written templates, and it scales infinitely to any chart and transit combination. The investment that would have gone into writing templates goes into crafting, testing, and iterating this prompt instead.

**PROMPT STRUCTURE**

The system prompt has four sections. First, a role and voice definition that establishes the psychological, confrontational, warm tone with explicit instructions on what to avoid (astrological jargon, vague reassurances, generic advice). Second, a set of five to ten gold-standard example entries, hand-written to anchor the exact quality and style the model should match. These examples are the most important part of the prompt — they define the ceiling. Third, the output format specification requesting structured JSON with specific fields for each calendar entry and the chart summary. Fourth, the user data payload containing the natal chart JSON, transit list, context answers, and first name.

**VOICE GUARDRAILS**

The prompt should explicitly prohibit several patterns that LLMs default to when writing about astrology or personal development: no cosmic or mystical language ("the universe is telling you"), no passive hedging ("you may feel" — instead, "you will feel"), no generic positivity ("trust the process"), no astrological terminology in output text, and no more than one question per calendar entry description. The prompt should also instruct the model to vary sentence structure across entries and avoid repetitive openings.

**GOLD-STANDARD EXAMPLES**

The hand-written examples should cover a range of transit types and emotional registers: one entry that is confrontational and uncomfortable, one that is warm and encouraging, one that is practical and action-oriented, and one that is reflective and slow. The model will interpolate between these reference points based on the specific transit, producing varied output that never feels formulaic. Writing these five to ten examples well is the single most important creative task before launch.

**TESTING AND ITERATION**

Before launch, the prompt should be tested against at least twenty different chart and context combinations. The test criteria: does every entry sound like the same voice? Does the output avoid astrological language completely? Are the entries specific enough to feel personal? Do the descriptions vary in length and structure across a full calendar? Is the chart summary a genuine "how does it know" moment? Prompt refinement should be iterative — run a batch, identify the weakest entries, adjust the prompt, run again.

# 8. Development Priorities

Ordered by what unblocks the next thing. The build sequence is designed so that each phase produces something testable.

**PHASE 1: FOUNDATION (DAYS 1–3)**

Set up the Next.js project in Cursor with the brand’s typography and colour system. Build the questionnaire flow as a multi-step form with smooth transitions. No backend yet — the form submits to a local state object. This phase is done when the questionnaire feels right: the pacing, the micro-copy, the typography, the transitions.

**PHASE 2: CHART ENGINE (DAYS 4–7)**

Integrate Swiss Ephemeris. Build the API route that takes birth data and returns a chart JSON. Build the chart summary page with the SVG wheel. Generate a test chart and verify accuracy against a known ephemeris source. The chart summary text can use placeholder content at this stage. This phase is done when you can fill out the form and see your own chart displayed correctly.

**PHASE 3: AI INTEGRATION (DAYS 8–12)**

Build the transit calculation engine. Write and iterate the system prompt for Claude Sonnet 4.6. Build the API route that sends chart data and transits to Claude and parses the structured response. Wire the AI-generated chart summary into the chart page. Generate the CSV output from the AI response. Wire the email capture. This is the longest phase because prompt engineering requires iteration — expect to go through multiple rounds of generate, review, refine. This phase is done when you can go from the questionnaire to a downloaded CSV file with calendar entries that genuinely impress you.

**PHASE 4: POLISH AND SHIP (DAYS 13–16)**

Responsive testing, mobile refinement, loading states (the AI generation takes a few seconds — this needs to feel intentional, not broken), error handling, edge cases (unknown birth time, unusual locations, API failures). Deploy to Vercel. Get a domain. This phase is done when you can send someone a link and they can use it end to end.

# 9. Risk Mitigation

**API RELIABILITY**

Claude Sonnet 4.6 is a production API but any external dependency can fail. The MVP should handle API errors gracefully with a retry mechanism and a user-facing message ("We’re taking a moment to read your chart carefully — please wait" on slow responses, and a fallback screen if the call fails entirely). Consider caching the AI response briefly server-side so that if a user refreshes the chart page, it does not trigger a new API call.

**VOICE CONSISTENCY**

The primary risk with AI-generated content is inconsistency — some entries landing perfectly while others feel generic or off-brand. Mitigation: invest heavily in the gold-standard examples in the system prompt, test across many chart types before launch, and set a temperature parameter that balances creativity with consistency (likely 0.7–0.8). Post-launch, monitor the output quality by periodically generating test calendars and reviewing them.

**SCOPE CREEP**

The known risk for this project. The spec deliberately draws a hard line around what ships in the MVP. If something is not in the feature scope table under "MVP," it does not get built before launch. The temptation to add accounts, add payment, add a richer chart explorer, or polish the content pipeline before shipping the core product should be actively resisted. The goal is a live URL with working chart-to-calendar flow, not a complete platform.

# 10. Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if it achieves three things. First, someone can go from landing on the site to having a transit calendar in their Google Calendar in under three minutes. Second, the AI-generated calendar entries make the user feel seen — the "how does it know" reaction should happen for at least some entries in every calendar generated. Third, the brand experience is consistent and distinctive enough that the site itself is worth sharing, independent of whether the person believes in astrology.

The metric that matters most at launch is not traffic or signups. It is whether people screenshot the calendar entries and share them. If the AI interpretations are good enough to screenshot, the product works and the content strategy writes itself.
