**WHAT CHANGED SINCE v0.2**

- Calendar output is an ICS file, not CSV (Apple Calendar does not import CSV).
- Ephemeris is Astronomy Engine, not Swiss Ephemeris. House system is Whole Sign.
- The AI work is split into two parallel calls (chart summary, calendar) instead of one. One large call would keep the user waiting a minute or more on the chart page.
- The chart summary is now a short read plus three pattern → rule cards, linked to points on the wheel.
- The calendar gains twelve monthly context cards, one on the 1st of each month.
- Dates always come from the transit engine. The model writes text only and never outputs dates.
- Repeated passes of the same transit (retrograde loops) are handled explicitly.
- Unknown birth time also excludes the Moon from dated transits, not only the Ascendant.
- Model name comes from an environment variable. Geocoding, timezones and email capture providers are fixed (see section 6).

# 1. Product Vision

A web-based personal development tool that translates astrological transit data into psychologically grounded, actionable calendar entries. The user never needs to understand astrology. They experience a timeline of honest, personal insight mapped to their year, delivered in a voice that feels like a friend who studied psychology and happens to know their chart.

The astrology is the engine. The psychology is the interface. The calendar is the delivery mechanism.

The flow is fully self-serve. Nothing is sent to the person by a human or on a delay. They answer a few questions, see their chart and a short read of who they are, and download their year.

**CORE POSITIONING**

Existing astrology apps fall into two camps: full mystical language (Susan Miller, Cafe Astrology) or cold brutalist aesthetics (Co–Star). Neither speaks to the growing audience that is interested in self-awareness tools but turned off by horoscope culture. This product occupies the gap: warm, serif, Japanese-inspired minimalism with a voice that is honest, slightly confrontational, and deeply personal. No zodiac jargon in the output. Astrology in the hashtags and metadata only.

**KEY DIFFERENTIATOR**

Every calendar entry and every line of the chart summary is generated by Claude, synthesising the user's full natal chart, the specific transit, and their life context answers into a unique interpretation. This is not template-based horoscope content. Two people with the same sun sign but different moon placements, rising signs, and life situations will receive meaningfully different output. The AI layer is what makes the product feel personal rather than generic. It is the product.

The calculation is never done by the model. All positions, aspects and dates are computed in code and handed to the model as structured data. The model only interprets.

# 2. Brand Identity

**VOICE**

Honest, warm, slightly confrontational. Like a therapist who skips the pleasantries. The tone assumes intelligence, avoids hand-holding, and names the uncomfortable thing directly. It is personal without being precious, actionable without being prescriptive.

*Instead of:* "Saturn transiting your 7th house — a period of testing in partnerships."

*We say:* "The next few months are going to pressure-test your closest relationships. You'll feel the urge to either commit harder or walk away — that tension is the point. Ask yourself: am I staying because this is real, or because leaving feels harder?"

**VISUAL IDENTITY**

Serif typography (the product should feel literary, not techy). Japanese-inspired minimalism: generous whitespace, warm paper tones, restraint over decoration. The palette is off-white and warm neutrals with a single muted accent. Think Kinfolk magazine meets a personal journal. The entire experience, from an Instagram post to the website to a calendar entry sitting in someone's Google Calendar, should feel like the same voice and the same warmth.

**CONTENT STRATEGY HOOK**

Social content (Instagram, TikTok) uses the same psychological voice as headlines but includes astrology tags and hashtags for discoverability. The posts read as personal development, the metadata reads as astrology. This self-selects for the right audience: people searching astrology content find something that feels different from everything else in that space.

# 3. MVP Scope

The MVP is scoped to be buildable in two to three focused weeks using Cursor with Claude as the AI coding assistant. It ships the smallest version that delivers real value and feels like the brand from the first interaction. The principle: a beautifully paced three-step flow that gives you an AI-generated personal calendar is more valuable than a feature-rich tool that feels generic.

**WHAT SHIPS**

Three screens:

1. A multi-step birth data questionnaire, with two optional life context questions.
2. A chart page: a minimal natal chart wheel, a short read of the person, and three pattern → rule cards.
3. A calendar download: an ICS file with twelve months of AI-written transit entries plus twelve monthly context cards, importable into Google Calendar and Apple Calendar.

**WHAT DOES NOT SHIP**

User accounts and saved charts, payment or premium tiers, the paid natal reading and other deeper documents, any Vedic or sidereal layer, a full interactive chart explorer, the content automation pipeline (n8n, social posting), a mobile app, and the dating/synastry concept. These are future layers (see `docs/roadmap-v2.md`), not MVP requirements.

# 4. User Flow

## Step 1: Birth Data Questionnaire

A clean, single-question-per-screen form inspired by Typeform. The pacing matters. Each screen should feel calm and considered, not like a data entry form. Fields collected:

- Date of birth (typed day / month / year fields)
- Time of birth (typed hour / minute fields, with an "I don't know" option)
- Place of birth (location autocomplete; returns coordinates and IANA timezone)
- First name only (used to personalise the output)
- Two optional context questions (see below)

**OPTIONAL CONTEXT QUESTIONS**

These are not astrological. They add a life-context layer that makes the AI output feel personal rather than generically insightful. Presented as optional single-select screens after the birth data. Tapping an option auto-advances; no Continue button.

- "What area of your life feels most in motion right now?" Options: Relationships, Career, Identity, Health, Creativity.
- "What's your relationship with change?" Options: I seek it out, I resist it, It depends, I'm in the middle of one.

Both stay single-select. A multiselect on the first question would dilute the signal to the level of skipping it.

The two answers do different jobs in the prompt:

- **Focus area** decides which life domains entries lean toward. The category name itself is never echoed back to the user.
- **Change stance** shapes the tone and judgment of the advice (push, steady, reassure), not its topic.

The second question's subtitle can reference the first answer to make the flow feel responsive, without collecting new data.

**UNKNOWN BIRTH TIME**

If the user selects "I don't know my birth time," the system builds a solar chart: planets at noon local time, the Sun's sign as house 1, Whole Sign houses from there. The chart page says this plainly, in brand voice: who you are still holds; where in your life it plays out is left out rather than guessed; adding the time later unlocks it.

With an unknown birth time:

- The Ascendant is excluded from transit targets (it sits on the Sun, so it would duplicate the Sun's transits).
- The Moon is excluded from dated transit targets. Over a day the Moon moves about 13°, so a noon position can be up to ~6.5° off, which moves the date of a slow transit by weeks or months. The Moon can still inform the chart summary in general terms.
- House-based detail is left out entirely: transits carry `house: null`, and the prompts are told not to name life areas. Skipping is more honest than guessing.

## Step 2: Chart Page

After submission, the user lands on the chart page. This is not a chart explorer. It is a moment of recognition. The goal is for the user to read a few sentences and think "how does it know."

**LOADING ORDER**

1. The chart is computed in code and the wheel renders immediately. No AI wait for the wheel.
2. Two AI calls start in parallel as soon as the chart exists:
   - **Summary call:** small and fast. Fills the read and the pattern cards on this page.
   - **Calendar call:** larger and slower. Runs in the background while the person reads the summary and enters their email.
3. By the time they reach the download, the calendar is usually ready. If not, the download area shows a calm, intentional waiting state ("Still writing your year…") and becomes active when it is.

**WHAT APPEARS**

**The wheel.** A clean, minimal SVG (see section 6, Chart Visual). The points the summary talks about are emphasised. Everything else is quiet.

**The read.** Three to four sentences that synthesise the person's core configuration. This is the "how does it know" moment. Example for someone with Sun in Scorpio, Moon in Gemini and Ascendant in Libra:

*"You lead with charm and diplomacy — people experience you as easy, balanced, agreeable. Underneath that, you're running two competing programs: one that needs to control and transform everything it touches, and one that can't stop asking questions and gets bored the moment it has an answer. The tension between depth and lightness is your operating system."*

**Three pattern → rule cards.** Each card names one way the person operates and gives one concrete rule for working with it. This is the action layer: it turns "here's who you are" into "here's how to work with it." Each card references the chart points it comes from, and the wheel shows a matching marker on those points.

Example card:

- **Pattern:** You treat a decision as unfinished until you've found the option nobody could criticise. That option rarely exists, so you stall.
- **Rule:** Give yourself a deadline you say out loud to someone. When it hits, pick the option you'd defend to a friend, not to a judge.

**EMAIL CAPTURE**

Below the summary, before the download, a single email field: "Get your calendar, and we'll let you know when new transits hit your chart." A short consent line sits next to it (EU/GDPR), in brand voice. No account creation. This is the growth mechanism.

## Step 3: Calendar Download

The primary output: an ICS file covering the next twelve months, built from two kinds of entries.

**1. DATED TRANSIT ENTRIES**

One all-day event per exact transit hit. Each has a title (the psychological headline) and a description (two to four sentences of actionable, personal insight). No astrological terminology in the user-facing text.

Example:

- **Date:** June 14, 2027
- **Title:** Your patience with half-truths runs out today
- **Description:** Something you've been tolerating in a relationship or at work is going to feel suddenly intolerable. This isn't impulsive — you've been building toward this clarity for weeks. Follow the frustration. It's telling you where your standards actually are.

**Repeated passes.** Slow planets often make the same aspect two or three times in a year because of retrograde motion. Each exact hit is its own dated entry, and the engine tags it with its pass number and total passes. The prompt uses this: the first pass introduces the theme, a middle pass revisits it ("this is back, and it's asking the question again"), the final pass closes it ("last time this comes up for a while — decide").

**2. MONTHLY CONTEXT CARDS**

Twelve all-day events, one on the 1st of each month (the first card is dated on the download day if it falls after the 1st). Each card is the month's weather: which longer themes are active, what the month is for, one thing to watch. Two to four sentences.

These exist because of a real calendar limitation: events that span weeks or months take up space in every week of the month view and bury the dated entries. One card per month carries the background instead.

The card's input is computed in code: every transit within orb at any point during that month, plus any outer planet stations (turning retrograde or direct) that month.

**TRANSIT SELECTION FOR MVP**

- Transiting planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto.
- Natal targets: Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Ascendant (with the unknown-birth-time exclusions above).
- Aspects: conjunction, opposition, square. Major aspects only.
- Window: twelve months from the download date.

Expected volume (estimate, depends on the chart): roughly 15–35 dated entries a year including repeated passes, plus the 12 monthly cards. If a chart produces only a handful of dated hits, do not pad the calendar. The monthly cards carry the year.

# 6. Technical Architecture

**STACK**

Next.js (App Router, TypeScript strict) deployed on Vercel. Tailwind CSS v4. Built in Cursor with Claude as the AI coding assistant. The exact decisions and providers are in `.cursor/rules/project.mdc`.

**CHART CALCULATION**

Astronomy Engine (MIT, pure JS) computes tropical, geocentric ecliptic longitudes. All calls go through one adapter (`src/lib/astro/ephemeris.ts`) so the backend can be swapped for Swiss Ephemeris later. Ascendant and MC are computed in our own code. Houses are Whole Sign.

The flow: birth date, time, place → local time converted to UTC with `luxon` using the IANA zone from the geocoder (Open-Meteo) → planetary positions, Ascendant, MC, houses, natal aspects → one chart JSON.

That chart JSON is the single source of truth. The wheel, the summary call, the transit engine and the calendar call all read from it. Nothing recomputes or re-enters natal degrees anywhere else. (A natal degree that differs between two outputs produces transit dates that disagree with each other.)

Keep raw ecliptic longitudes in the chart JSON, not only signs and houses. Future layers (e.g. a sidereal view) are derived from them.

**TRANSIT ENGINE**

**Naming (one scheme everywhere):** `PlanetId` = `sun | moon | mercury | venus | mars | jupiter | saturn | uranus | neptune | pluto`. `PointId` = `PlanetId | asc | mc`. `AspectId` = `conjunction | opposition | square` (natal aspects may add more). The chart JSON, the transit engine, the summary's `pointIds` and the wheel all use these same IDs, so a card can be matched to its dots without translation.

A function that takes the chart JSON and finds every exact hit in the next twelve months for the selection in section 4. For each hit it produces a structured object:

```ts
{
  id: string;            // stable, e.g. "saturn-square-sun-2027-03-14"
  date: string;          // YYYY-MM-DD, exact hit (local to the user's birth zone is fine for all-day events)
  transiting: PlanetId;  // jupiter | saturn | uranus | neptune | pluto
  natal: PointId;        // sun | moon | mercury | venus | mars | asc
  aspect: AspectId;      // conjunction | opposition | square
  house: number | null;  // Whole Sign house of the natal point; null when birth time is unknown
  pass: number;          // 1-based
  totalPasses: number;   // passes of this same transit within the window
}
```

It also produces, per month, the list of transits in orb and any outer planet stations. Those feed the monthly cards.

Exact hits are found by searching for the moment the angular separation crosses the exact aspect angle, not by sampling daily and picking the closest day. Retrograde motion produces multiple crossings; each is one hit.

Verify the engine against Astro-Seek or astro.com transit listings for at least two charts before writing prompts against its output.

**AI INTERPRETATION LAYER**

Two calls to Claude, both server-side in route handlers, started in parallel from the chart page. Model name from `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`. Output is JSON, validated with `zod` before use.

*Summary call* (`src/lib/ai/outputs/summary.ts`)

- Input: chart JSON, context answers, first name.
- Output:

```ts
{
  read: string;                 // 3–4 sentences
  patterns: Array<{             // exactly 3
    pointIds: PointId[];        // 1–3 chart points this pattern comes from
    pattern: string;            // 1–2 sentences
    rule: string;               // 1–2 sentences, concrete and doable
  }>;
}
```

*Calendar call* (`src/lib/ai/outputs/calendar.ts`)

- Input: chart JSON, transit list, monthly context data, context answers, first name.
- Output:

```ts
{
  entries: Array<{ transitId: string; title: string; description: string }>;
  months: Array<{ month: string /* YYYY-MM */; title: string; description: string }>;
}
```

The model never outputs dates. Entries are joined back to the engine's transit objects by `transitId`, and the date comes from the engine. Validation fails if an ID is missing, unknown or duplicated, or if a month is missing. On failure: retry once, then show the fallback.

One prompt module per output type, sharing a common voice block and the gold-standard examples in `src/lib/ai/examples/`. Future paid documents are added as new modules next to these, not by growing these two.

**Route timing.** The calendar call can take a minute or more. Set `export const maxDuration` on that route and check it against the current Vercel plan's function duration limit.

**Refresh and caching.** With no database and serverless functions, server memory is not a reliable cache. Store both AI results in `sessionStorage`, keyed by a hash of the birth data and context answers (wrapped in try/catch). A refresh restores them instead of calling the API again.

**CALENDAR FILE**

The ICS file is built by a pure function in `src/lib/calendar/` from the engine's dated transits joined with the model's text, plus the monthly cards. It contains no secrets, so the client can build it from the calendar call's response and trigger the download as a Blob. No server-side storage is needed between generation and download. Format rules are in `project.mdc` (RFC 5545, all-day events, CRLF, folding, escaping, stable UIDs).

**COST**

Two calls per user. Rough size: summary ~2k input / ~0.8k output tokens; calendar ~4k input / ~4–7k output tokens, depending on the number of entries. At Sonnet-class pricing this lands around ten cents per user or less. Verify against current pricing before launch; it should not constrain product decisions either way.

**CHART VISUAL**

An SVG wheel drawn from the chart JSON. Rendering it in the browser is fine; it doesn't need to be server-rendered. Not interactive. Direction (proposed, pending the design pass):

- Emphasise only the points the summary talks about (the `pointIds` from the pattern cards). Everything else is quiet.
- Small matching markers link each pattern card to its points on the wheel.
- No astrological glyphs in user-facing labels; glyphs count as jargon. If points are labelled, use plain words.
- The unknown-birth-time version drops the twelve house dividers and shows planets on a plain ring. Design it early so it doesn't look like a broken version of the full wheel.

**HOSTING AND INFRASTRUCTURE**

Vercel. No database for the MVP: data lives in the browser session between submission and download. Email capture goes to Buttondown through a route handler. Geocoding goes through a route handler to Open-Meteo (free tier is non-commercial; revisit before any paid launch).

# 7. System Prompt Architecture

The prompts are the single highest-leverage asset in the product. They replace what would otherwise be ninety-plus hand-written templates, and they scale to any chart and transit combination.

**PROMPT STRUCTURE**

Each output type has its own system prompt, built from shared parts:

1. **Voice block (shared).** The psychological, confrontational, warm tone, with explicit prohibitions.
2. **Gold-standard examples (shared library, selected per output).** From `src/lib/ai/examples/`. These define the ceiling.
3. **Output spec (per output).** The JSON shape and field rules above.
4. **User payload.** Chart JSON, transits or monthly data, context answers, first name.

**VOICE GUARDRAILS**

No cosmic or mystical language ("the universe is telling you"). No passive hedging ("you may feel"; write "you will feel"). No generic positivity ("trust the process"). No astrological terminology in output text. At most one question per description. Vary sentence structure and openings across entries. Never echo the context answer labels back ("since you chose Career…"). Never assume what the engine can't know: no weekdays ("before Friday"), seasons or hemisphere ("in the spring"), holidays or weather.

**GOLD-STANDARD EXAMPLES**

The library covers a range of registers: confrontational, warm, practical, reflective, a repeated pass, a final pass, and monthly cards. Plus examples of the summary read and pattern → rule cards. Each example is annotated with the internal transit it represents (internal only). The model interpolates between these reference points.

The starting set is written. It should be revised by hand before launch: this is still the most important creative task in the project.

**TESTING AND ITERATION**

Before launch, test against at least twenty chart and context combinations, including unknown birth time and a chart with few transit hits. Criteria: one consistent voice; zero astrological language; specific enough to feel personal; varied length and structure across a calendar; repeated passes read as a sequence, not duplicates; the summary is a genuine "how does it know" moment; every pattern card has a rule a person could actually do this week. Iterate: run a batch, find the weakest outputs, adjust, run again. Save a golden baseline once quality is acceptable.

# 8. Development Priorities

Ordered by what unblocks the next thing. Each phase produces something testable.

**PHASE 1: FOUNDATION**

Next.js project with the brand's typography and colour tokens. The questionnaire as a multi-step form with calm transitions. Done when the questionnaire feels right: pacing, micro-copy, typography, transitions.

**PHASE 2: CHART ENGINE**

Ephemeris adapter, timezone conversion, chart calculation route, the chart page with the SVG wheel. Verify against Astro-Seek or astro.com for at least three known charts. Summary text can be placeholder. Done when you can fill out the form and see your own chart correctly.

**PHASE 3: AI INTEGRATION**

Transit engine (with repeated passes and monthly data), verified against a known source. Summary call wired into the chart page. Calendar call running in the background. ICS generation and download. Email capture. Prompt iteration against the examples library. This is the longest phase. Done when you can go from the questionnaire to an imported calendar whose entries genuinely impress you.

**PHASE 4: POLISH AND SHIP**

Responsive testing, loading and waiting states, error handling, edge cases (unknown birth time, DST edges, unusual locations, API failures, slow calendar generation). Deploy. Domain. Done when you can send someone a link and they can use it end to end.

# 9. Risk Mitigation

**API RELIABILITY**

Any external dependency can fail. Retry once, then show a graceful fallback. The chart page must stay useful if the calendar call fails: the wheel and summary remain, and the download area offers a retry.

**SLOW GENERATION**

The calendar call is the slowest step. It starts the moment the chart exists, runs while the person reads, and the download waits for it with an intentional state. Never block the chart page on it.

**VOICE CONSISTENCY**

Some outputs land perfectly while others feel generic. Mitigation: invest in the examples library, test across many chart types, and keep temperature moderate (likely 0.7–0.8). Post-launch, periodically generate test calendars and review them.

**CALCULATION ACCURACY**

The model never calculates. The engine is verified against an external source before prompts are written against it, and the chart JSON is the only source of natal degrees.

**SCOPE CREEP**

The known risk for this project. If something is not marked MVP in the feature scope table, it does not get built before launch. Paid documents, Vedic layers, payment and accounts all live in `docs/roadmap-v2.md` until the MVP is live. The goal is a live URL with a working chart-to-calendar flow, not a complete platform.

# 10. Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if it achieves three things. First, someone can go from landing on the site to having a transit calendar in their Google Calendar in under three minutes. Second, the AI output makes the user feel seen: the "how does it know" reaction should happen for at least some entries in every calendar, and at least one pattern card should feel uncomfortably accurate. Third, the brand experience is consistent and distinctive enough that the site itself is worth sharing, independent of whether the person believes in astrology.

The metric that matters most at launch is not traffic or signups. It is whether people screenshot the pattern cards and calendar entries and share them. If the interpretations are good enough to screenshot, the product works and the content strategy writes itself.
